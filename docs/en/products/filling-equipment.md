# Filling and Packaging Equipment Selection & Parameter Hub

![Integrated filling and packaging line](/images/filling-solutions/filling-packages.webp)

Use this hub to move from a packaged-liquid requirement to the correct rinsing, filling, closing, inspection, labeling and end-of-line equipment. The published values below and on each product page are references for initial comparison, not unconditional output or accuracy guarantees. A manufacturer quotation should be based on the actual liquid, containers, closures, line interfaces and acceptance method.

## Define these eight inputs before comparing prices

| Purchasing input | Information to provide | Why it changes the configuration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liquid | Product name, viscosity, temperature, foaming tendency, particles and food or chemical duty | Determines the filling principle, contact materials, pump, valves and cleaning method |
| Bottle or container | Material, nominal volume, height, body diameter, neck finish, stability and representative samples | Determines guides, star wheels, nozzles, conveyors and format parts |
| Closure | Cap, press-on cap, tamper-evident cap, cork or shrink capsule; dimensions and feed condition | Determines the cap feeder, pressing, rolling, corking, sealing or shrinking station |
| Label and code | Label material, bottle surface, label position, date or batch-code content and substrate | Determines labeling, inspection and laser-selection requirements |
| Sustained line rate | Required bottles per hour for each format, plus upstream supply and downstream packing rate | Prevents one module from becoming the line bottleneck |
| Changeover | Number of SKUs, change frequency, acceptable downtime and retained-product limit | Affects tooling, adjustment method, cleaning and operator workload |
| Cleaning | Product-change procedure, flush or CIP expectation, drainage, hygiene zoning and contact-material requirement | Affects piping, tank, valve, guarding and documentation scope |
| Complete-line boundary | Required tanks, pumps, conveyors, rinser, filler, closer, dryer, labeler, coder, case packer, palletizer and utilities | Makes supplier quotations comparable and exposes excluded interfaces |

A fully automatic line is not automatically the best purchase. A semi-automatic or modular line can be more practical when formats change often, batches are small or local labor and maintenance capability favor simpler equipment. Automation should follow the operating case, not replace it.

## Compare manufacturer quotations on the same boundary

- Identify the base machine, format parts, conveyors, guards, controls and product-contact materials included in the quoted price.
- State which tanks, pumps, piping, utilities, cable runs, foundations and upstream or downstream signals are supplied by each party.
- Separate optional modules, first-use consumables, recommended spare parts and future-format tooling from the base price.
- Confirm export packing, delivery term, destination, documentation language and estimated dispatch basis in writing.
- Define whether installation, commissioning, operator training and production support are remote, on site or excluded.
- Record the warranty, after-sales response, travel, accommodation and replacement-part terms exactly as offered; do not assume a duration or free service.

## FAT, SAT and acceptance planning

FAT should use approved samples or a documented substitute and should cover the agreed format range, sustained operating run, changeover, cleaning, safety interlocks and line communication. SAT should verify the installed utilities, interfaces and agreed acceptance method at the destination. Throughput, fill result, closure result, label position, rejection logic and allowed stoppages must be written into the purchase specification before testing.
