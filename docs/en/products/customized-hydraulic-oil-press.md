## Conditions That Must Be Verified

### Edible Oil Materials
- Raw-material identity, moisture, laboratory oil content, particle size, temperature, and pretreatment
- Batch load, pressure program, pressing time, oil collection method, filtration, and number of repeated tests
- Oil yield definition, residual oil in cake, oil quality targets, and laboratory methods
- Required food-contact materials, cleaning procedure, and applicable local compliance requirements

### Residues, Waste, and Sludge
- Full composition, solids content, initial moisture range, viscosity, particle size, contaminants, corrosiveness, and SDS
- Required cake moisture or volume-reduction target, including the sampling and laboratory method
- Filtrate handling, odor or emissions controls, cake discharge, cleaning, and disposal responsibilities
- Material variability, expected batch cycle, consumables, and compatibility of seals, filter cloth, and contact parts

### Capacity and Utilities
- Rated pressure and structural design confirmed by drawings, calculations, and machine nameplate
- Effective batch load confirmed using the actual material bulk density rather than barrel volume alone
- Daily capacity calculated from verified batch load, complete cycle time, cleaning time, shifts, and planned availability
- Electrical load, heating demand, cooling, compressed air, water, drainage, floor loading, and installation clearances

## Customization Options

### 1. Equipment Specifications
- **Pressure Class**: Select within the available 300-1000 ton range after engineering review
- **Barrel Size**: Match diameter and height to material behavior and verified batch requirements
- **Equipment Dimensions**: Coordinate the footprint and service clearances with the site layout

### 2. Functional Configuration
- **Automation**: Manual, semi-automatic, or line-integrated controls based on the operating sequence
- **Temperature Control**: Select heating and monitoring functions for the agreed process window
- **Filtration**: Size filtration stages against test results, target clarity, viscosity, and throughput

### 3. Special-Material Configuration
- **Multi-Barrel Layout**: Double-barrel, triple-barrel, or parallel units based on cycle analysis
- **Contact Materials**: Select corrosion resistance and food-contact grade from the material composition and local requirements
- **Site and Environmental Interfaces**: Define power, drainage, ventilation, filtrate handling, and other controls in the final design

## Configuration Scenarios

### Premium Edible Oil Batch Pressing
Possible configuration: hydraulic press, material preparation equipment, temperature monitoring, filtration, and protected oil storage.

Verify before selection: actual oil content and moisture, pretreatment, batch cycle, oil yield calculation, residual oil, clarity, flavor or quality tests, and cleaning requirements.

### High-Impurity or Special-Material Pressing
Possible configuration: compatible contact materials, selected seals, controlled pressure program, staged filtration, and easier-cleaning discharge paths.

Verify before selection: chemical compatibility, impurity range, plugging behavior, filtrate quality, consumable life, cleaning time, and safety controls.

### Sludge or Residue Dewatering
Possible configuration: large or multiple barrels, selected filter cloth, filtrate collection, mechanical discharge, and site-specific guarding.

Verify before selection: feed variability, initial and final moisture test methods, cake handling, cycle time, filtrate treatment, odor or emissions requirements, and disposal compliance. No moisture-reduction percentage should be treated as guaranteed without a representative witnessed trial and written acceptance criteria.

## Customization and Acceptance Process

1. **Requirements Review**: Document material properties, target output, site conditions, utilities, safety constraints, and required records.
2. **Sample Trial**: Agree on the test protocol, witness method, measurements, repeated batches, and acceptance criteria.
3. **Engineering Proposal**: Issue an itemized configuration, layout, utility list, exclusions, and preliminary drawings for review.
4. **Production and Factory Acceptance**: Inspect the completed equipment and run the agreed no-load and material tests with recorded results.
5. **Delivery and After-Sales**: Define installation, commissioning, training, warranty, spare parts, maintenance, response times, and site services in the contract. Ongoing technical support is available within the agreed support arrangement; other after-sales obligations follow the contract scope.
