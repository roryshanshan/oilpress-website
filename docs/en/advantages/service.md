# Service and Support

## Define the Service Scope Before Ordering

Service availability depends on the equipment, destination, project schedule, local access, personnel, spare parts, and signed commercial terms. The quotation and contract should identify what is included, excluded, remote, on site, chargeable, or provided by a third party.

### Pre-Sale Engineering Review

- **Material review**: Record material type, moisture, oil content or solids content, contaminants, temperature, particle size, and safety information.
- **Equipment selection**: Compare candidate models and configurations against a representative sample trial, site utilities, required output, and acceptance criteria.
- **Proposal**: Provide an itemized configuration, assumptions, exclusions, layout, utility list, test plan, delivery scope, and commercial terms.

### Order and Production Support

- **Contract documents**: Confirm the technical agreement, final configuration, drawings, payment milestones, delivery terms, and change-control process.
- **Production milestones**: Agree which progress records, photos, document reviews, or witness points will be supplied.
- **Factory acceptance**: Define no-load checks, safety checks, material tests, records, corrective actions, and sign-off responsibilities.

### Delivery and After-Sales

- **Installation and commissioning**: State whether these are remote or on site, who provides lifting, utilities, civil work, travel, accommodation, tools, and consumables.
- **Operator training**: Define trainees, language, duration, topics, documents, and completion record.
- **Ongoing technical support**: Provide support through the channels and service hours stated in the contract.
- **Spare parts and maintenance**: Identify recommended parts, prices or pricing method, lead times, shipping terms, maintenance tasks, and responsibility for wear parts.

## Service Delivery Model

Remote diagnosis can be provided where the customer supplies clear photos, video, alarm history, electrical readings, hydraulic readings, and recent operating conditions. On-site service may be arranged directly or through an agreed local provider when included in the contract and when travel, access, visa, safety, and parts conditions allow.

Do not rely on an unverified number of service outlets or a general statement of worldwide coverage. For a specific destination, request the service contact, legal entity or authorized provider, location, coverage area, personnel capability, and escalation path in writing.

## Response and Escalation Terms

The contract or service-level schedule should define:

- Support channels, service hours, time zone, languages, and required information for opening a case
- Severity levels and acknowledgement targets for safety, stopped-production, degraded-operation, and general questions
- Remote diagnosis steps, customer responsibilities, data access, and expected update frequency
- Conditions for dispatching personnel, estimated travel basis, visa and site-access dependencies, and chargeable costs
- Spare-parts availability, shipment method, customs responsibility, and temporary workaround process
- Closure criteria, unresolved-case escalation, warranty decision, and service record retention

Fixed acknowledgement, arrival, or resolution times should not be stated unless they are included in the signed terms for the destination and supported by the required personnel and parts arrangement.

## Return and Order Issue Handling

- Equipment is supplied against the factory quotation, technical confirmation, and signed contract. Confirm voltage, model, configuration, capacity assumptions, packing, delivery, and service scope before production.
- For transport damage, quantity discrepancies, or mismatch with the confirmed order, preserve packaging, take dated photos and video, note the issue on the carrier record where possible, and contact the named contract representative promptly.
- Warranty coverage, repair, replacement, return, freight, customs, travel, labor, and consumables follow the contract and applicable mandatory law.
- Keep the machine nameplate, serial or order number, operating record, alarms, maintenance history, and evidence requested for diagnosis.
